\section{Green Functions and the Retarded Solution}
\label{sec:ch02.4}

\subsection{Scalar Green function from the Helmholtz equation}
\label{sec:ch02.4.0}

In a homogeneous vacuum region, each Cartesian component of the vector potential in Lorenz gauge satisfies the scalar Helmholtz equation with source $-\mu_0 J_i$, where $J_i$ is the $i$th component of $\Jimp$ (A/m$^2$) \cite{stratton1941,jackson1999}. The same template governs acoustic monopoles and serves as the building block for vector radiation. For a scalar source density $q(\mathbf{r}')$ (units depend on the physical quantity modeled), the inhomogeneous equation reads
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ch2.scalar-helmholtz-green-pde}
\big(\nabla^{2}+k_0^{2}\big)\,\phi(\mathbf{r})=-q(\mathbf{r}),
\end{equation}
where $\phi(\mathbf{r})$ is the scalar response at observation point $\mathbf{r}$ (m), $k_0=\omega\sqrt{\mu_0\varepsilon_0}$ is the vacuum wavenumber (rad/m), and $\nabla^2$ is the Laplacian in $\mathbf{r}$. The Green function $G(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}')$ is defined as the response to a unit point source at $\mathbf{r}'$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ch2.scalar-green-def}
\big(\nabla^{2}+k_0^{2}\big)\,G(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}')=-\delta(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}'),
\end{equation}
with $\delta$ the three-dimensional Dirac distribution. Superposition then gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ch2.scalar-green-convolution}
\phi(\mathbf{r})=\int_{\Omega_s} G(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}')\,q(\mathbf{r}')\,d^{3}r',
\end{equation}
where $\Omega_s$ is the source support and $d^3r'$ is the volume element at the source point $\mathbf{r}'$. Physically, Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.scalar-green-convolution} accumulates spherical waves launched from every source element; $G$ tells how strongly a source at $\mathbf{r}'$ influences $\mathbf{r}$.

For an unbounded exterior with outgoing selection at infinity, the unique spherical solution of Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.scalar-green-def} for $\mathbf{r}\neq\mathbf{r}'$ is Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.scalar-green-free-space}. The amplitude $1/(4\pi R)$ with $R=|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}'|$ is geometric spreading in three dimensions; the phase $e^{-\jj k_0 R}$ is retarded propagation under the $+\jj\omega t$ convention adopted in this chapter. The homogeneous Helmholtz equation also admits the incoming partner $\exp(+\jj k_0 R)/R$; localized sources plus causality exclude that branch (Section~\ref{sec:ch02.4.2}).

\subsection{Why a dyadic Green function is required}
\label{sec:ch02.4.1}

A point electric current dipole oriented along $\hat{\mathbf{a}}$ produces, in general, both $\E$- and $\H$-components along directions other than $\hat{\mathbf{a}}$ because Maxwell's equations couple the vector components through curl. The field--source relation is therefore \emph{tensorial}: each Cartesian component of $\E(\mathbf{r})$ depends on all three components of $\Jimp(\mathbf{r}')$. The scalar Green function $G(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}')$ solving $(\nabla^2+k_0^2)G=-\delta(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}')$ suffices only for the Lorenz-gauge potential of a scalar source; for the full vector problem one introduces the dyadic Green function $\boldsymbol{\Gamma}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}')$ such that the electric field from an impressed current distribution is \cite{stratton1941,harrington2001}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ch2.E-from-dyadic}
\E(\mathbf{r})=-\jj\omega\mu_0\int_{\Omega_s}\boldsymbol{\Gamma}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}')\cdot\Jimp(\mathbf{r}')\,d^3r',
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{r}$ is the observation point, $\mathbf{r}'$ the source point, $\Omega_s=\operatorname{supp}\Jimp$, and $d^3r'$ the volume element (m$^3$). Equation~\eqref{eq:ch2.E-from-dyadic} is the frequency-domain statement of superposition: each infinitesimal current element radiates with outgoing phase and amplitude determined by $\boldsymbol{\Gamma}$.

The dyadic satisfies the vector wave equation with a unit-strength dipole source at $\mathbf{r}'$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ch2.dyadic-green-pde}
\curl\curl\boldsymbol{\Gamma}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}')-k_0^2\boldsymbol{\Gamma}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}')=
\mathbf{I}\,\delta(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}'),
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{I}$ is the $3\times3$ identity dyad. The singularity at $\mathbf{r}=\mathbf{r}'$ is treated in Subsection~\ref{sec:ch02.4.3}; the operator derivation appears in Subsection~\ref{sec:ch02.4.1b}. The scalar outgoing kernel in vacuum satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ch2.scalar-green-free-space}
G_0(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}')=\frac{e^{-\jj k_0 R}}{4\pi R},
\qquad
R=|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}'|,
\end{equation}
for $R>0$, with $k_0$ from Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.k0-eta0}. Equation~\eqref{eq:ch2.scalar-green-free-space} solves $(\nabla^2+k_0^2)G_0=-\delta(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}')$ and represents a unit-strength monopole source radiating an outgoing spherical wave \cite{stratton1941,jackson1999}. Amplitude decay as $1/R$ and phase $e^{-\jj k_0 R}$ are the geometric signatures of propagation at distance $R$ under the $+\jj\omega t$ convention.

In Lorenz gauge, the vector potential $\mathbf{A}$ (T$\cdot$m) driven by $\Jimp$ reduces each Cartesian component to the scalar Helmholtz equation with source $-\mu_0\Jimp$. The electric field is recovered without abandoning Maxwell coupling:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ch2.lorenz-E-from-A}
\E(\mathbf{r})=+\jj\omega\mu_0\,\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{r})
+\frac{1}{+\jj\omega\varepsilon_0}\,\grad\,\divg\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{r}),
\end{equation}
after which $\H=(\curl\E)/(-\jj\omega\mu_0)$ from Faraday's law in Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch1.maxwell-local} \cite{stratton1941,harrington2001}. For impressed-current excitation, the electric dyadic $\overline{\mathbf{G}}_{E}$ is often more direct than $\mathbf{A}$ because it maps $\Jimp$ to $\E$ in one convolution \cite{harrington2001}. In free space with outgoing selection, the electric dyadic may be written in the compact form \cite{stratton1941,jackson1999}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ch2.dyadic-free-space}
\overline{\mathbf{G}}_{E}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}')
=\Big(\mathbf{I}+\frac{1}{k_0^2}\grad\grad-\frac{\jj}{k_0}\hat{\mathbf{R}}\times\big\Big)\,
\frac{e^{-\jj k_0 R}}{4\pi R},
\qquad
\hat{\mathbf{R}}=\frac{\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}'}{R},
\end{equation}
with $R=|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}'|>0$. Equation~\eqref{eq:ch2.dyadic-free-space} satisfies Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.dyadic-green-pde} for $\mathbf{r}\neq\mathbf{r}'$ and carries the outgoing phase required by Section~\ref{sec:ch02.4.2}. The $\grad\grad$ term generates longitudinal response tied to charge accumulation; the $\hat{\mathbf{R}}\times$ term enforces transverse radiation in the far zone.

In the time domain, causality requires $\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}',t-t')=\mathbf{0}$ for $t-t'<0$. The harmonic phasor $\boldsymbol{\Gamma}$ is the Fourier transform of that retarded impulse under the $+\jj\omega$ convention \cite{stratton1941}.

\subsection{The outgoing radiation condition}
\label{sec:ch02.4.2}

In an unbounded homogeneous exterior, the homogeneous Helmholtz equation admits both $\exp(-\jj k_0 r)/r$ and $\exp(+\jj k_0 r)/r$ spherical solutions. A localized source launched from finite support must select the branch whose phase fronts move outward with increasing $r$ in the time domain---equivalently, the factor $\exp(-\jj k_0 r)$ with harmonic time dependence $\eim$ \cite{stratton1941}. Sommerfeld's radiation condition makes this precise: for scalar $\psi$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ch2.sommerfeld-scalar}
\lim_{r\to\infty} r\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial r}+\jj k_0\right)\psi(\mathbf{r})=0,
\end{equation}
with $r=|\mathbf{r}|$. For vector fields, the transverse radiative part of $\E$ and $\H$ must exhibit the same outgoing $k_0 r$ phase \cite{stratton1941,harrington2001}.

The operator $\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{out}}$ enforces this selection on completed field pairs: it annihilates incoming propagating content while leaving outgoing propagating content unchanged on the physical sheet. Composed with $\Pi_{\mathrm{prop}}$, which removes evanescent and non-propagating spectral parts from far-zone radiation claims, it defines $\Pi_{\mathrm{rad}}$ through Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.Pirad-composition}. On finite computational domains, absorbing boundaries approximate $\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{out}}$; spurious reflections mean the numerical map is only an approximation to true outgoing radiation \cite{hansen1988}.

Uniqueness: for fixed admissible $\Jimp$ and outgoing exterior condition, the Maxwell solution $\mathcal{S}_\omega[\Jimp]$ is unique \cite{stratton1941}. Radiative extraction then yields a unique $\mathcal{R}_\omega[\Jimp]$ in $\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{rad}}$.
