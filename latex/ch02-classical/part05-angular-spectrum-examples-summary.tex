\section{Physical Examples}
\label{sec:ch02.11}

\subsection{Electrically small dipole}
\label{sec:ch02.11.1}

The Hertzian dipole is developed in Section~\ref{sec:ch02.5.4}: dipole moment $\mathbf{p}$, distance regimes, far-zone transverse field Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.dipole-far-E}, and radiated power Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.dipole-P-rad}. That calculation is the reference against which every larger source model must reduce at $k_0 a\ll1$ \cite{jackson1999,chu1948}.

\subsection{Non-radiating loop pair}
\label{sec:ch02.11.2}

Two equal and opposite current loops separated by a distance small compared with $\lambda_0$ can produce strong local $\H$ while canceling the leading dipole radiation moment. The far-zone pattern meters read zero: the configuration lies in (or near) $\ker\mathcal{R}_\omega$ for ideal cancellation \cite{devaney2004,harrington2001}. Adding any small asymmetry breaks exact nullity; the example shows why ``large near field'' does not imply ``strong radiation.''

\subsection{Small magnetic dipole loop}
\label{sec:ch02.11.3}

A closed wire loop of area $A$ (m$^2$) carrying phasor current $I_0$ (A) at $k_0\ell\ll1$ for loop perimeter $\ell$ supports magnetic dipole moment $\mathbf{m}=\hat{\mathbf{n}}\,I_0 A$ (A$\cdot$m$^2$), where $\hat{\mathbf{n}}$ is the loop normal. The far-zone field is transverse with a characteristic $\sin\theta$ pattern orthogonal to $\mathbf{m}$, distinct from the electric dipole pattern of Section~\ref{sec:ch02.5.4} \cite{jackson1999,stratton1941}. Power and momentum export follow Eqs.~\eqref{eq:ch2.flux-on-radiative-branch}--\eqref{eq:ch2.far-zone-momentum-flux} on $\mathcal{R}_\omega[\Jimp]$; the loop is a standard calibration of magnetic multipole order at low $k_0 a$.

\subsection{Propagating and evanescent sheets}
\label{sec:ch02.11.4}

On plane $z=0$, a spectral component with $k_x^2+k_y^2\le k_0^2$ has real $k_z$ and propagates along $z$ (Section~\ref{sec:ch02.10.1}). A component with $k_x^2+k_y^2>k_0^2$ has imaginary $k_z$: amplitude decays as $e^{-|k_z||z|}$ and does not contribute to $P_{\mathrm{rad}}(r)$ at infinity \cite{stratton1941,harrington2001}. Applying $\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{lat}}$ to $\mathcal{S}_\omega[\Jimp]$ without $\Pi_{\mathrm{rad}}$ can show large $k_t>k_0$ amplitudes that are reactive storage, not radiative export---a common misreading of near-field scan data \cite{hansen1988,devaney2004}.

\subsection{Incoming versus outgoing homogeneous waves}
\label{sec:ch02.11.5}

In source-free homogeneous regions, $\exp(-\jj k_0 r)/r$ and $\exp(+\jj k_0 r)/r$ both satisfy the Helmholtz equation. Only the outgoing branch matches a localized source and causal retarded excitation (Subsections~\ref{sec:ch02.2.5} and~\ref{sec:ch02.4.2}). A standing-wave superposition may satisfy Maxwell's equations locally yet fail the radiation condition; $\Pi_{\mathrm{rad}}$ and $\mathcal{R}_\omega$ are defined to retain outgoing propagating export, not arbitrary standing patterns \cite{stratton1941,jackson1999}.

\section{Summary and Passage to Chapter~\ref{ch:03}}
\label{sec:ch02.12}

This chapter developed the source-to-radiation chain for admissible impressed currents. Maxwell's equations determine the complete field $\mathcal{S}_\omega[\Jimp]$; outgoing selection $\Pi_{\mathrm{rad}}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{out}}\circ\Pi_{\mathrm{prop}}$ extracts the radiative component; $\mathcal{R}_\omega$ maps sources to that component; finite measurements implement $\Qomega$ with rank and null-space limitations. Non-radiating sources and reciprocity constrain inversion and coupling. The angular spectrum describes directional radiative content before harmonic bases are chosen.

Chapter~\ref{ch:03} addresses the natural successor question: how should angular structure on the sphere be labeled and organized by rotational symmetry through vector spherical harmonics and angular-momentum operators? Chapter~\ref{ch:04} develops realizability and effective dimension of sources that produce prescribed radiative fields.

\subsection{Results inherited by later chapters}
\label{sec:ch02.12.1}

The radiation operator $\mathcal{R}_\omega$, null space $\ker\mathcal{R}_\omega$, observation map $\Qomega$, and plane-wave angular spectrum are the primary exports to Chapters~\ref{ch:03}--\ref{ch:04}. Maxwell consistency, admissibility, and Poynting flux remain anchored in Chapter~\ref{ch:01} and are used here by reference \cite{maxwell1873,stratton1941}.
