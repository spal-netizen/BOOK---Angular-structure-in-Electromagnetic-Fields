\section{Green Functions and Electromagnetic Inversion}
\label{sec:ch23}

Section~\ref{sec:ch22} fixed the harmonic Maxwell operator $\mathcal{L}_\omega$ and the source-to-field map $\mathcal{S}_\omega$ on domains with declared boundary data. The present section answers the propagation question those operators presuppose: what field does a localized impressed current produce at an observation point $\mathbf{r}$ when the medium response is known through its Green function \cite{stratton1941}.

In homogeneous vacuum the scalar kernel inverts $(\nabla^{2}+k_0^{2})$; the electric dyad inverts the curl--curl operator $\mathcal{W}_{E,\omega}=\nabla\times\nabla\times-k_0^{2}$ that appears in the electric-wave form of Maxwell's equations under $\eim$ \cite{jackson1999,tai1994}. Subsection~\ref{sec:ch231} derives the outgoing vacuum kernels; Subsection~\ref{sec:ch232} treats coincidence, distributions, and regularization so convolution integrals are defined on realistic source supports; Subsection~\ref{sec:ch233} records retarded time-domain support. Subsection~\ref{sec:ch234} addresses inversion and solvability on the outgoing branch. The Hertzian dipole remains the elementary calibration: it images $\mathcal{S}_\omega[\Jimp]$ when $\Jimp$ collapses to a moment, with transverse far-zone pattern developed in Subsection~\ref{sec:ch23.signature-dipole} \cite{jackson1999}.

Outgoing branch selection, Sommerfeld radiation, and uniqueness of the exterior radiative solution belong to Section~\ref{sec:ch24}; until then $\mathcal{R}_\omega=\Pi_{\mathrm{rad}}\circ\mathcal{S}_\omega^{(\mathrm{out})}$ is cited only as the target export map \cite{sommerfeld1949}.
