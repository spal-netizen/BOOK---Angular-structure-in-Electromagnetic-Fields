\section{Green Functions and Electromagnetic Inversion}
\label{sec:ch23}
Section~\ref{sec:ch22} fixed the harmonic Maxwell operator $\mathcal{L}_\omega$ and the
source-to-field map $\mathcal{S}_\omega$ on bounded domains with declared boundary data.
Section~\ref{sec:ch23} answers the propagation question those operators presuppose: what field
does a localized impressed current produce at an observation point $\mathbf{r}$ when the medium
response is known through its Green function \cite{stratton1941}.
In homogeneous vacuum the scalar kernel inverts $(\nabla^{2}+k_0^{2})$; the electric dyad inverts
the curl--curl operator $\mathcal{W}_{E,\omega}=\nabla\times\nabla\times-k_0^{2}$ that appears in the
electric-wave form of Maxwell's equations under $\eim$ \cite{jackson1999,tai1994}.
Subsection~\ref{sec:ch231} derives the outgoing vacuum kernels; Subsection~\ref{sec:ch232} treats
coincidence, distributions, and regularization so convolution integrals are defined on realistic source
supports; retarded time-domain support belongs to Subsection~\ref{sec:ch233}.
The same propagation law underlies method-of-moments matrix entries, near-field scan
back-propagation, and compact-range transforms---only the discretization differs \cite{harrington2001}.
A Hertzian dipole at $k_0 a\ll 1$ remains the lowest-dimensional calibration: it images
$\mathcal{S}_\omega[\Jimp]$ when $\Jimp$ collapses to a moment, with transverse far-zone pattern fixed by
Subsection~\ref{sec:ch23.signature-dipole} \cite{jackson1999}.
Outgoing branch selection, Sommerfeld radiation, and uniqueness of the exterior radiative solution belong to
Section~\ref{sec:ch24}; until then $\mathcal{R}_\omega=\Pi_{\mathrm{rad}}\circ\mathcal{S}_\omega^{(\mathrm{out})}$
is cited only as the target export map, not re-derived here \cite{sommerfeld1949}.
