\section{Summary}
\label{sec:ch02.11}

Chapter~\ref{ch:02} developed harmonic Maxwell theory as an operator chain from impressed sources
to measurable radiative data. Impressed currents $\Jimp$ on bounded support, together with
declared boundary data and charge continuity inherited from Chapter~\ref{ch:01}, determine a
harmonic field pair through the solution map $\mathcal{S}_\omega$. Outgoing Sommerfeld
selection and far-amplitude extraction $\Pi_{\mathrm{rad}}$ isolate the transverse content
that carries net export to infinity; the radiation map
$\mathcal{R}_\omega=\Pi_{\mathrm{rad}}\circ\mathcal{S}_\omega$ is the object to which power,
pattern, and reciprocity integrals attach \cite{maxwell1873,stratton1941,sommerfeld1949}.

Function spaces and domain declarations in Section~\ref{sec:ch21} made curl, divergence, and
tangential trace legitimate operations. Time--frequency equivalence in Section~\ref{sec:ch22}
showed that $\mathcal{S}_\omega$ is the $\omega$-slice of causal retarded evolution when media,
sources, and boundary class are fixed. Green convolutions in Section~\ref{sec:ch23} realize
$\mathcal{L}_\omega^{-1}$ on exteriors; Sommerfeld selection and uniqueness in Section~\ref{sec:ch24}
fix the outgoing branch. Radiation integrals in Section~\ref{sec:ch25} evaluate fields in near,
intermediate, and far regimes and separate reactive storage from conserved radiated power.

Spectral structure, null spaces of $\mathcal{R}_\omega$, reciprocity, and angular-spectrum
filters in Sections~\ref{sec:ch26}--\ref{sec:ch29} explain why finite geometry and finite
observation rank compress accessible degrees of freedom without altering Maxwell's local laws
\cite{chu1948,devaney2004,harrington2001}. Worked configurations in Section~\ref{sec:ch210}
illustrate the distinction between field existence, radiative export, and finite recoverability:
electrically small sources suffer spectral compression; non-radiating currents share patterns with
radiating partners; evanescent energy can dominate near shells while far amplitudes remain fixed;
balanced modal superpositions may exhibit vivid phase structure with vanishing signed transport
\cite{stratton1941}.

Vector spherical harmonics, realizability bounds, and formal interaction theory beyond this
chapter are developed in Chapters~\ref{ch:03} and~\ref{ch:04} by reference, without local
re-derivation here \cite{harrington2001}. The central lesson for the remainder of Volume~I is
that every angular or transport claim must state whether it refers to $\mathcal{S}_\omega[\Jimp]$,
to $\mathcal{R}_\omega[\Jimp]$ after outgoing selection, or to a finite-rank observation of
that radiative image \cite{stratton1941}.
