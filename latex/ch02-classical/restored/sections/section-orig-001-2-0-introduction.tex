\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:ch20}

At a fixed angular frequency, radiation theory asks two coupled questions: given an impressed current on a bounded support, what electromagnetic field does Maxwell's theory determine, and given outgoing field data observed on a finite aperture, what can legitimately be inferred about the source that produced that radiation \cite{harrington2001,stratton1941}? The first is a forward boundary-value problem with sources, constitutive laws, and radiation conditions stated explicitly; the second is an inverse problem constrained by null spaces, finite observation geometry, and measurement limits that cannot be removed by rearranging field formulas \cite{devaney2004}. This chapter develops the harmonic theory that keeps impressed data, Maxwell closure, reactive storage, outgoing radiation, and observability in the correct order \cite{maxwell1873}.

Chapter~\ref{ch:01} fixed the local laws and admissibility of a Maxwell problem on a declared domain: Eqs.~\eqref{eq:ch1.maxwell-local} and~\eqref{eq:ch1.charge-continuity-harmonic} under the volume convention $\eim$ with $\partial_t\to+\jj\omega$ on phasors. The present chapter does not postulate new curl equations. It derives how bounded impressed currents $\Jimp$ drive $(\E,\H)$ through Green representations and outgoing selection at infinity, then records the far-zone observables---Poynting flux, patterns, and compressed angular data---that laboratories actually measure \cite{stratton1941,sommerfeld1949,poynting1884}. Historically, exterior solutions became computable through retarded Green kernels and Sommerfeld's radiation condition; Poynting's surface integral turned power through a closed sphere into a primary quantity; work on finite electrical size showed that bounded sources cannot export arbitrary angular complexity at fixed bandwidth \cite{chu1948,harrington2001}.

The scientific progression is therefore physical before symbolic. One specifies admissible impressed sources and boundary data compatible with Chapter~\ref{ch:01}; Maxwell's equations determine $(\E,\H)$, including reactive and stored structure near the source; on open exteriors, Sommerfeld-outgoing selection singles out the propagating branch whose transverse fields carry net export to infinity; surface and far-zone integrals define radiative observables. Only after these steps are in place does the chapter introduce compact names for the maps already derived---the harmonic solution map $\mathcal{S}_\omega:\mathcal{J}_\Omega\to\mathcal{F}_\Omega$ and the radiation data extracted from the far-zone limit of $\mathcal{S}_\omega[\Jimp]$ \cite{stratton1941}. The selector $\Pi_{\mathrm{rad}}$ is not assumed to be a global orthogonal projection on all of $\mathcal{F}_\Omega$; its domain, action, and limitations are stated where outgoing asymptotics are established (Subsections~\ref{sec:ch201} and~\ref{sec:ch292}).

A coaxial stub and a center-fed dipole in the same cell at $1\,\mathrm{GHz}$ may show comparable probe magnitude on a small scan surface, yet only the open structure sustains net outward power through a large enclosing sphere once outgoing qualification is enforced \cite{stratton1941,harrington2001}. Reactive diagnostics belong to the full Maxwell solution; wattmeter and pattern readings belong to the outgoing asymptotic data after the appropriate far-zone screen from Chapter~\ref{ch:01}. Three structural facts recur: not every Maxwell-consistent field radiates; not every radiated component is recoverable from finite aperture and finite signal-to-noise ratio; and radiative equivalence of far patterns does not imply uniqueness of $\Jimp$ \cite{devaney2004,stratton1941}.

Subsections~\ref{sec:ch201}--\ref{sec:ch203} connect this physical chain to source admissibility and export criteria inherited from Chapter~\ref{ch:01}. Section~\ref{sec:ch21} supplies function spaces and operator domains; later sections develop time--frequency equivalence, Green functions, uniqueness and inversion limits, flux integrals, spectral structure, null spaces, reciprocity, and angular spectra in the order required by the forward derivation above.
