\subsection{Radiation as a source-to-field operator}
\label{sec:ch201}

A network analyzer $|S_{11}|$, a spherical near-field scan on $\Gamma_R$, and a far-zone pattern cut sample different points on one physical chain: impressed data, Maxwell closure on $(\E,\H)$, identification of outgoing asymptotics, and finite observation \cite{maxwell1873,stratton1941}. Misplacing a measurement on that chain---for example, reporting reactive storage as radiated power---remains a common compact-range failure mode \cite{poynting1884,hansen1988}.

Section~\ref{sec:ch20} treated radiation as a question of \emph{accessibility} of outgoing data, not merely of field existence. Maxwell's equations in Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch1.maxwell-local} specify the complete field pair driven by admissible impressed sources; only after Sommerfeld-outgoing selection is the transverse far-zone fraction that carries net export singled out for pattern and wattmeter interpretation \cite{stratton1941,sommerfeld1949}. The formal solution and radiation maps introduced below are concise labels for those already-stated physical steps.

Laboratory measurements presuppose an excitation model even when it is left implicit: a port
drive, a probe sample, or a termination fixes how energy is partitioned between storage and
escape \cite{stratton1941}. Impressed data from Section~\ref{sec:ch01.1.2} map through Maxwell's
equations to \((\E,\H)\), and only then is the outward-propagating asymptotic data
identified as the carrier of far-zone power and angular flux
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch1.maxwell-local}; Sections~\ref{sec:ch01.1.4}--\ref{sec:ch01.1.5}). At fixed
$\omega$, $\mathcal{S}_\omega$ denotes the harmonic solution map and $\mathcal{R}_\omega$ the
corresponding far-zone radiation data under the Chapter~\ref{ch:01} convention $\eim$ with
$\partial_t\to+\jj\omega$ \cite{stratton1941}.

Let \(\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}\) be a piecewise smooth domain with boundary
\(\Gamma=\partial\Omega\). Phasor fields \(\E(\mathbf{r})\) and \(\H(\mathbf{r})\)
are suppressed in time unless causality is restored below. Impressed electric
current \(\Jimp\), charge density \(\rho\), and the data tuple \(\mathfrak{D}\) of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch1.data-specification-tuple} are not redefined; they are the
inputs on which the maps act. Finite-source admissibility is assumed in the sense of the
\ref{ch:01} $\mathcal{D}_J=1$ conditions
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch1.continuity-admissibility}): only configurations with
\(\mathcal{D}_{J}=1\) enter the domain of the radiation chain developed here
\cite{stratton1941}.

Function spaces are declared before the maps. Let $\mathcal{F}_\Omega$ be the
space of phasor pairs \((\E,\H)\) on \(\Omega\) whose components have
\(L^{2}\)-integrable curls and divergences in the sense required by
Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch1.maxwell-operator-block}, and whose boundary traces lie in the
domain of \(\mathcal{B}_\Gamma\). Let \(\mathcal{J}_\Omega\subset L^{2}(\Omega)^{3}\)
be the impressed-current space compatible with finite-energy class
(Section~\ref{sec:ch01.0.3}), charge continuity
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch1.charge-continuity-harmonic}), and the same boundary class.
Refined Sobolev and distributional extensions of these spaces are supplied in
Section~\ref{sec:ch211}; here the declaration is minimal but precise enough to
state the maps \cite{stratton1941}. The harmonic Maxwell \emph{solution map} is the
first quantitative object in the radiation chain: it records, for each admissible impressed
current \(\Jimp\in\mathcal{J}_\Omega\), the Maxwell field pair that the governing
equations and boundary data jointly permit. Physically, this map is what a full-wave solver
computes when it inverts Maxwell's equations on a declared domain; the output is a \emph{complete} field state, not yet a radiative report
\cite{stratton1941}. Experimentally one would observe, on a closed measurement surface
enclosing the source, both reactive circulation and outward-propagating flux in the
solution unless outgoing selection is applied afterward \cite{harrington2001}. The solution
map sends impressed data to a field state:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ch2.maxwell-solution-map}
\mathcal{S}_\omega:\mathcal{J}_\Omega\longrightarrow\mathcal{F}_\Omega,
\qquad
\Jimp\longmapsto(\E,\H),
\end{equation}
defined by \(\mathcal{S}_\omega[\Jimp]=(\E,\H)\) satisfying the Maxwell operator
residuals of Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch1.maxwell-operator-block} with sources \(\mathfrak{D}\)
and boundary data \(\mathbf{g}_\Gamma\) \cite{stratton1941}.
Equation~\eqref{eq:ch2.maxwell-solution-map} is the Chapter~\ref{ch:02} statement
of the initial-boundary value problem proven in Section~\ref{sec:ch01.1.1}; it
does not alter the law, only the input--output viewpoint.
Injectivity fails in general: distinct \(\Jimp\) can produce identical \((\E,\H)\)
on observational subdomains, and equivalent source distributions related by
divergence-free reparametrizations inside \(\Omega\) can leave exterior patterns
unchanged when \(\mathcal{B}_\Gamma\) permits reactive storage
(Section~\ref{sec:ch01.2.4}). Those equivalences are not nuisances to be removed
by definition; they foreshadow null-space and realizability analyses in
Sections~\ref{sec:ch27} and \ref{ch:04}. Radiation accounting therefore
never begins from a guessed modal field; it begins from \(\mathcal{S}_\omega[\Jimp]\)
with explicit completion.
The full state typically contains both stored and exported electromagnetic energy.
Reactive fields exchange power with sources and terminations at distances small
compared with \(k_0^{-1}\); radiative fields carry net outward flux at infinity
in open configurations. For $(\E,\H)\in\mathcal{F}_\Omega$ on an exterior domain, the far-zone amplitude $\mathbf{E}_0(\theta,\phi)$ is defined when
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ch2.far-amplitude-limit}
\mathbf{E}(\mathbf{r})
=
\mathbf{E}_{0}(\theta,\phi)\,\frac{e^{-\jj k_0 r}}{r}
+o(r^{-1}),
\qquad r\to\infty,
\end{equation}
holds, with $\mathbf{H}=\eta_0^{-1}\hat{\mathbf{r}}\times\mathbf{E}$ at leading order as in Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch1.radiation-trace-condition} \cite{stratton1941,sommerfeld1949}. Convergence is taken on spheres $\Gamma_R=\{|\mathbf{r}|=R\}$: the tangential trace of $\mathbf{E}$ approaches $\mathbf{E}_0(\theta,\phi)e^{-\jj k_0 R}/R$ in $L^{2}(\Gamma_R)$ as $R\to\infty$, uniformly on angular sectors fixed before taking the limit \cite{jackson1999}. Two pairs are radiatively equivalent when they share the same far-zone amplitude:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ch2.radiative-equivalence}
(\E_a,\H_a)\sim_{\mathrm{rad}}(\E_b,\H_b)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\mathbf{E}_{0,a}(\theta,\phi)=\mathbf{E}_{0,b}(\theta,\phi)
\ \text{a.e.\ on }S^{2},
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{E}_{0,a}$ is the limit in Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.far-amplitude-limit} for $(\E_a,\H_a)$ when that limit exists \cite{stratton1941}. The space $\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{rad}}$ is the set of equivalence classes $[(\E,\H)]_{\mathrm{rad}}$ under $\sim_{\mathrm{rad}}$. The \emph{asymptotic extraction map}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ch2.Pirad-map-def}
\Pi_{\mathrm{rad}}:\mathcal{D}(\Pi_{\mathrm{rad}})\subset\mathcal{F}_\Omega
\longrightarrow\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{rad}},
\qquad
(\E,\H)\longmapsto [(\E,\H)]_{\mathrm{rad}},
\end{equation}
is defined on the domain
$\mathcal{D}(\Pi_{\mathrm{rad}})=\{(\E,\H)\in\mathcal{F}_\Omega:\text{Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.far-amplitude-limit} holds}\}$;
it records far-zone data, not a subtraction of near fields from $(\E,\H)$ \cite{stratton1941}. It is not an orthogonal projection on $\mathcal{F}_\Omega$. On $\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{rad}}$, $\Pi_{\mathrm{rad}}$ is the identity on equivalence classes; on $\mathcal{F}_\Omega$, applying $\Pi_{\mathrm{rad}}$ to a single representative does not generally return the same $(\E,\H)$ unless the near field already vanishes on the stated class.

The \emph{radiation operator} is well-defined on currents whose solutions lie in $\mathcal{D}(\Pi_{\mathrm{rad}})$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ch2.radiation-operator-def}
\mathcal{R}_\omega:\mathcal{J}_\Omega\longrightarrow\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{rad}},
\qquad
\mathcal{R}_\omega[\Jimp]=\Pi_{\mathrm{rad}}\,\mathcal{S}_\omega[\Jimp].
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:ch2.radiation-operator-def} composes $\mathcal{S}_\omega$ with asymptotic extraction. Section~\ref{sec:ch242} connects $\Pi_{\mathrm{rad}}$ to outgoing Green kernels and Sommerfeld selection.

\begin{proposition}[Existence and representative independence of $\Pi_{\mathrm{rad}}$]
\label{prop:ch2.Pirad-well-defined}
Assume $\Jimp\in\mathcal{J}_\Omega$ with compact support $\Omega_s\Subset\mathbb{R}^{3}$, homogeneous lossless vacuum in the exterior, outgoing Sommerfeld condition as in Section~\ref{sec:ch241}, and $\mathcal{S}_\omega[\Jimp]\in\HCurl_{\mathrm{loc}}$ satisfying Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch1.maxwell-local}. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The limit in Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.far-amplitude-limit} exists: there is $\mathbf{E}_0\in L^{2}(S^{2})$ such that $\|\mathbf{E}|_{\Gamma_R}-\mathbf{E}_0 e^{-\jj k_0 R}/R\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_R)}\to 0$ as $R\to\infty$.
\item $\mathcal{S}_\omega[\Jimp]\in\mathcal{D}(\Pi_{\mathrm{rad}})$, so $\mathcal{R}_\omega$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.radiation-operator-def} is defined on all admissible $\Jimp$ under these hypotheses.
\item If $(\E_a,\H_a)\sim_{\mathrm{rad}}(\E_b,\H_b)$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.radiative-equivalence}, then $\Pi_{\mathrm{rad}}[(\E_a,\H_a)]=\Pi_{\mathrm{rad}}[(\E_b,\H_b)]$; the map $\Pi_{\mathrm{rad}}:\mathcal{D}(\Pi_{\mathrm{rad}})\to\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{rad}}$ is constant on $\sim_{\mathrm{rad}}$-classes.
\item On $\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{rad}}$, $\Pi_{\mathrm{rad}}$ is the identity map on classes: $\Pi_{\mathrm{rad}}[(\E,\H)]=[(\E,\H)]_{\mathrm{rad}}$ and $\Pi_{\mathrm{rad}}\circ\Pi_{\mathrm{rad}}=\Pi_{\mathrm{rad}}$ when the composition is read on equivalence classes, not as a map $\mathcal{F}_\Omega\to\mathcal{F}_\Omega$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Existence.} For compact $\Jimp$, the outgoing retarded representation of Section~\ref{sec:ch231} yields $\E(\mathbf{r})$ analytic in $|\mathbf{r}|$ for $|\mathbf{r}|>\max_{\mathbf{r}'\in\Omega_s}|\mathbf{r}'|$ and admits the multipole expansion
$\E(\mathbf{r})=\sum_{\ell,m}(\mathbf{M}_{\ell m}a_{\ell m}+\mathbf{N}_{\ell m}b_{\ell m})$ with outgoing Hankel functions $h_\ell^{(1)}(k_0 r)$ \cite{stratton1941,jackson1999}. For large $r$, $h_\ell^{(1)}(k_0 r)\sim\mathrm{j}^{\ell+1}e^{-\jj k_0 r}/(k_0 r)$, so every term contributes at most $\mathcal{O}(r^{-1})$; the superposition defines $\mathbf{E}_0(\theta,\phi)=\lim_{r\to\infty} r e^{\jj k_0 r}\mathbf{E}(r\hat{\mathbf{r}})$ in $L^{2}(S^{2})$ \cite{stratton1941}.
\emph{Solution map in domain.} The same expansion proves Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.far-amplitude-limit} for $\mathcal{S}_\omega[\Jimp]$.
\emph{Representative independence.} If $\mathbf{E}_b=\mathbf{E}_a+\mathbf{w}$ with $\mathbf{w}|_{\Gamma_R}\in o(r^{-1})$ transverse on $\Gamma_R$, then $\|\mathbf{w}|_{\Gamma_R}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_R)}=o(1)$ forces $\mathbf{E}_{0,a}=\mathbf{E}_{0,b}$ under outgoing selection (uniqueness of the $1/r$ transverse coefficient; Section~\ref{sec:ch243}) \cite{sommerfeld1949}.
\emph{Idempotence on classes.} Extraction reads only $\mathbf{E}_0$; re-extraction leaves $[\mathbf{E}_0]$ unchanged. No inner product on $\mathcal{F}_\Omega$ is invoked \cite{stratton1941}.
\end{proof}

Causality constrains harmonic representations.
A frequency-domain phasor is physically meaningful only when it can be traced to a
retarded time-domain evolution that respects finite propagation speed.

current \(j(\mathbf{r},t)\) and retarded dyadic impulse
\(\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}',t-t')\),
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ch2.causality-retarded}
\E(\mathbf{r},t)
=
-\mu_0\,\frac{\partial}{\partial t}
\int
\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}',t-t')\cdot j(\mathbf{r}',t')\,d^{3}r'\,dt',
\end{equation}

\(\E(\mathbf{r},t)\) is the real time-domain electric field (V\,m\(^{-1}\));
\(j(\mathbf{r}',t')\) is the impressed current density (A\,m\(^{-2}\)) at source

\(1/r^{2}\) and \(1/r^{3}\) reactive terms; \(\mathcal{R}_\omega[\Jimp]\) retains
leading \(1/r\) transverse fields with \(kr\) phase in the far zone

Observability enters as functional composition rather than as a new postulate.
Let \(\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{far}}\) denote a linear functional that extracts a
declared far-zone scalar or vector observable---pattern voltage, axial ratio
proxy, or a single spherical-harmonic coefficient---from a field pair in
\(\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{rad}}\). The measured quantity associated with impressed current \(\Jimp\) is the scalar
\(\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{far}}\big[\mathcal{R}_\omega[\Jimp]\big]\), not
\(\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{far}}\big[\mathcal{S}_\omega[\Jimp]\big]\) unless the
observable has been shown to vanish on reactive content. Because both
\(\mathcal{R}_\omega\) and \(\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{far}}\) are linear on their
domains, the laboratory reading is a linear functional
\(\Lambda_{\mathrm{rad}}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{far}}\circ\mathcal{R}_\omega\)
on \(\mathcal{J}_\Omega\). That composition expresses in map language
the measurement limitations in Section~\ref{sec:ch01.5}: finite apertures and
finite modal sets appear as rank-deficient \(\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{far}}\), not
as modifications of Maxwell law \cite{stratton1941}. Angular-transport claims
require further that \(\Lambda_{\mathrm{rad}}\) be compatible with the flux
budgets of Section~\ref{sec:ch01.1.5} when the observable is interpreted as
momentum or power export.
On $\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{rad}}$, defined as pairs specified by their common outgoing far-zone coefficients, $\Pi_{\mathrm{rad}}$ is the identity map: extraction does not alter a state already in that class. On $\mathcal{F}_\Omega$, $\Pi_{\mathrm{rad}}$ is neither an orthogonal projection nor a near-field subtractor in general; two distinct near-field pairs can share identical asymptotic data, and uniqueness of the full pair requires the exterior BVP of Section~\ref{sec:ch243} \cite{stratton1941,sommerfeld1949}. Spatial windowing that truncates the near zone without enforcing the radiation condition therefore yields an approximation to $\Pi_{\mathrm{rad}}$, not the asymptotic map itself.
The composition law used downstream is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ch2.measurement chain-composition}
\mathcal{R}_\omega
=
\Pi_{\mathrm{rad}}\circ\mathcal{S}_\omega,
\qquad
\mathcal{S}_\omega:\mathcal{J}_\Omega\to\mathcal{F}_\Omega,
\quad
\mathcal{R}_\omega:\mathcal{J}_\Omega\to\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{rad}}.
\end{equation}
In Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.measurement chain-composition},
$\mathcal{S}_\omega$ is the Maxwell solution map, $\Pi_{\mathrm{rad}}$ the far-zone extraction map,
$\mathcal{R}_\omega=\Pi_{\mathrm{rad}}\circ\mathcal{S}_\omega$ the radiation map on admissible $\mathcal{J}_\Omega$, and the field spaces are those of Subsection~\ref{sec:ch211}.
Green kernels realize \(\mathcal{S}_\omega\); Sommerfeld conditions define
\(\Pi_{\mathrm{rad}}\); spectral theory in Section~\ref{sec:ch26} studies
eigenstructure on spaces refined in Section~\ref{sec:ch21}
\cite{harrington2001}. Nothing in
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:ch2.maxwell-solution-map}--\eqref{eq:ch2.measurement chain-composition}
re-proves results listed in Section~\ref{sec:ch01.7.1}; continuity
Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch1.continuity-map} is honored by citation-only continuity
of curl--curl algebra, stress flux theorems, and gauge lemmas.
Subsection~\ref{sec:ch201} has named \(\mathcal{S}_\omega\), \(\Pi_{\mathrm{rad}}\), and
\(\mathcal{R}_\omega\), introduced their Green and Poynting realizations, and connected
finite measurement rank to composition with far-zone observables. Subsection~\ref{sec:ch202}
identifies the Maxwell operator whose inversion, under declared boundary completion,
produces \(\mathcal{S}_\omega\).
