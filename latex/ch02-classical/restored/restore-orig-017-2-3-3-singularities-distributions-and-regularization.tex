\subsection{Singularities, distributions, and regularization}
\label{sec:ch232}
When observation points approach $\operatorname{supp}\Jimp$, the separation
$R=|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}'|$ is small and the free-space dyad exhibits inverse powers of $R$
that encode reactive self-interaction as well as radiation \cite{stratton1941}. Layered media
require the corresponding stratified Green function; the vacuum template below is the reference
kernel used to establish distributional identities and outgoing selection at infinity
\cite{collin2001,sommerfeld1949}.
Vacuum Maxwell equations under $\eim$ follow Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch1.maxwell-local}:
$\curl\E=-\jj\omega\mu_0\H$, $\curl\H=\Jimp+\jj\omega\varepsilon_0\E$, and $\nabla\cdot\Jimp=-\jj\omega\rho$
with impressed sources $(\Jimp,\rho)$ \cite{stratton1941,jackson1999}. Every distributional identity
below is verified in that coupled system with outgoing spatial phase $e^{-\jj k_0 R}$.
Three distinct mathematical objects must not be conflated \cite{yaghjian1978,tai1994}:
(i)~the tabulated dyad $\overline{\mathbf{G}}_{E,0}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}')$ for $R=|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}'|>0$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.free-space-dyadic-green};
(ii)~its distributional extension under $\mathcal{W}_{E,\omega}=\nabla\times\nabla\times-k_0^2$ tested against smooth $\mathbf{f}$;
(iii)~the electric field obtained by convolving $\Jimp$ with the kernel through a specified excluded principal volume $V_\varepsilon$ centred on the source point.
Unqualified $\delta$-function algebra on $\nabla\nabla(1/R)$ is inadequate: the electrostatic singular part of $G_0$ carries a longitudinal contact tensor when second derivatives act on $1/(4\pi R)$ \cite{yaghjian1978,yaghjian1980source}.
With $\mathbf{R}=\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}'$ and $R=|\mathbf{R}|$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ch2.grad-grad-1-over-R-distributional}
\partial_i\partial_j\frac{1}{4\pi R}
=
\mathrm{PV}\!\left[\frac{3R_iR_j-R^2\delta_{ij}}{4\pi R^{5}}\right]
-\frac{\delta_{ij}}{3}\,\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf{R}),
\end{equation}
under the spherical principal-value limit that excludes a ball $B_\varepsilon(\mathbf{r}')$ with outward normal on $\partial B_\varepsilon$
and $\varepsilon\to 0^{+}$ after testing against $\varphi\in C_c^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})$.
The isotropic contact term in Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.grad-grad-1-over-R-distributional} is the trace-one source dyad
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ch2.yaghjian-source-dyad-sphere}
\overline{\overline{L}}=\frac13\,\overline{\overline{I}}
\end{equation}
for that spherical geometry \cite{yaghjian1978}; other excluded volumes induce a different symmetric $\overline{\overline{L}}$ with unit trace and must not be identified with Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.yaghjian-source-dyad-sphere} without proof.
For the full Helmholtz kernel $G_0=e^{-\jj k_0 R}/(4\pi R)$, the $1/R$ singular structure supplies the contact piece in Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.grad-grad-1-over-R-distributional};
dynamic terms $\mathcal{O}(k_0 R)$ are less singular at coincidence and enter the excluded-sphere limit only through the regular expanding part of $G_0$.
The closed Lorenz--Stratton dyad Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.free-space-dyadic-green} already incorporates transverse and longitudinal coupling consistent with $\nabla\cdot\Jimp=-\jj\omega\rho$;
Theorem~\ref{thm:ch2.dyadic-distributional-fundamental} below states the corresponding curl--curl identity for that tabulated kernel, not for an uncorrected $\nabla\nabla G_0$ template analyzed without principal-value exclusion.
Convolution with extended $\Jimp$ uses the same spherical $V_\varepsilon$ convention as Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.dyadic-inner-sphere-flux-column}; one must not add an independent $\overline{\overline{L}}$ correction to a field already defined with the equivalent excluded-volume prescription \cite{yaghjian1978,tai1994}.
The electric dyadic $\overline{\mathbf{G}}_{E,0}$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.free-space-dyadic-green} maps an electric dipole
column to $\E$; the magnetic dyadic $\overline{\mathbf{G}}_{H,0}$ of Subsection~\ref{sec:ch231} inverts the magnetic curl
operator and must not be substituted in Theorem~\ref{thm:ch2.dyadic-distributional-fundamental} \cite{tai1994}. All
$\nabla$, $\nabla\times$, and $\nabla\times\nabla\times$ operations act on the observation coordinate $\mathbf{r}$ with
$\mathbf{r}'$ fixed when forming $\mathcal{W}_{E,\omega}\overline{\mathbf{G}}_{E,0}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}')$.
Subsection~\ref{sec:ch231} defined scalar and dyadic Green kernels that satisfy
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:ch2.scalar-helmholtz-green-pde} and~\eqref{eq:ch2.dyadic-green-defining-operator}
for \(\mathbf{r}\neq\mathbf{r}'\) and deferred coincidence evaluation to the present
subsection \cite{stratton1941}. Subsection~\ref{sec:ch232}
supplies the definitive account of distributional convolutions, principal-value
and finite-part prescriptions, static--dynamic kernel splits, and self-term
regularization that make Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.electric-dyadic-convolution} well-defined
on \(\Jimp\in\mathcal{J}_\Omega\) including sheet and filamentary support. The
Sobolev and distributional source classes of Subsection~\ref{sec:ch211} are presupposed
through Eqs.~\eqref{eq:ch2.distributional-source-extension} and~\eqref{eq:ch2.dual-source-pairing};
their functional-analytic construction is not repeated. Retarded time-domain support
belongs to Subsection~\ref{sec:ch233}; outgoing branch selection at infinity belongs
to Section~\ref{sec:ch24}. Let \(\Omega\Subset\mathbb{R}^{3}\) be Lipschitz with piecewise smooth internal
interfaces, \(\omega>0\) fixed, and \(\Jimp\) in the refined source class
Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.source-space-refined} or its distributional extension
Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.distributional-source-extension}. Kernels are the vacuum templates
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:ch2.free-space-scalar-green} and~\eqref{eq:ch2.free-space-dyadic-green}
unless \(\mathcal{B}_\Gamma\) or piecewise \(k(\mathbf{r})\) modify them patchwise.
Observation points \(\mathbf{r}\) may approach \(\operatorname{supp}\Jimp\) but
remain in the domain where \(\E\in\HCurl(\Omega)\) is sought.
Away from coincidence the kernels satisfy the homogeneous Helmholtz and curl--curl equations;
at coincidence the source appears as a distribution.
\begin{proposition}[Spherical principal value for $\partial_i\partial_j(4\pi R)^{-1}$]
\label{prop:ch2.grad-grad-PV-proof}
Let $\varphi\in C_c^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})$ and $\mathbf{R}=\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}'$.
With $V_a=\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus B_a(\mathbf{r}')$ and outward unit normal $\hat{\mathbf{n}}$ on $\partial B_a$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ch2.grad-grad-PV-pairing}
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\varphi(\mathbf{r})\,\partial_i\partial_j\frac{1}{4\pi R}\,d^{3}\mathbf{r}
=
\lim_{a\to 0^{+}}\int_{V_a}\varphi\,\partial_i\partial_j\frac{1}{4\pi R}\,d^{3}\mathbf{r}
=
\mathrm{PV}\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\varphi\,\frac{3R_iR_j-R^2\delta_{ij}}{4\pi R^{5}}\,d^{3}\mathbf{r}
-\frac{\delta_{ij}}{3}\,\varphi(\mathbf{r}'),
\end{equation}
which is Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.grad-grad-1-over-R-distributional} in test form.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $G_s=(4\pi R)^{-1}$ with $R=|\mathbf{R}|$ and $\mathbf{R}=\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}'$.
On $V_a=\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus B_a(\mathbf{r}')$, the integrand is smooth and
$\partial_i\partial_j G_s=(3R_iR_j-R^2\delta_{ij})/(4\pi R^5)$ for $R>a$.
Integrate by parts once on $V_a$:
\[
\int_{V_a}\varphi\,\partial_i\partial_j G_s\,d^{3}\mathbf{r}
=
-\int_{V_a}\partial_i\varphi\,\partial_j G_s\,d^{3}\mathbf{r}
+\oint_{\partial B_a}n_i\,\varphi\,\partial_j G_s\,dS,
\]
with $n_i$ the outward normal on $\partial B_a$ (pointing \emph{away} from the punctured ball).
A second integration by parts on $V_a$ transfers both derivatives onto $\varphi$:
\[
\int_{V_a}\partial_i\varphi\,\partial_j G_s\,d^{3}\mathbf{r}
=
-\int_{V_a}\varphi\,\partial_i\partial_j G_s\,d^{3}\mathbf{r}
+\oint_{\partial B_a}\partial_i\varphi\,n_j\,G_s\,dS,
\]
where the additional boundary term is $\mathcal{O}(a)$ because $\partial_i\varphi$ is bounded and $G_s=\mathcal{O}(a^{-1})$ integrates to $\mathcal{O}(a^{2})$ on the sphere.
Substituting into the first identity leaves only the leading $\oint n_i\varphi\partial_j G_s\,dS$ contact term plus the principal-value volume limit
$\lim_{a\to 0}\int_{V_a}\varphi\,(3R_iR_j-R^2\delta_{ij})/(4\pi R^5)\,d^{3}\mathbf{r}$, which defines $\mathrm{PV}$.
For $a$ small and $\varphi\in C_c^{\infty}$, $\varphi(\mathbf{r})=\varphi(\mathbf{r}')+\mathbf{R}\cdot\nabla\varphi(\mathbf{r}')+\mathcal{O}(R^{2})$ near $\mathbf{r}'$.
On $\partial B_a$, $\mathbf{R}=a\hat{\mathbf{n}}$, $\partial_j G_s=-\hat{n}_j/(4\pi a^2)$, and $n_i=\hat{n}_i$.
The volume term converges to the principal-value pairing against $(3R_iR_j-R^2\delta_{ij})/(4\pi R^5)$ as $a\to 0$; its limit defines $\mathrm{PV}$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.grad-grad-PV-pairing}.
The surface integral splits into the constant part and the linear part in $\mathbf{R}$:
\[
\oint_{\partial B_a}n_i\,\varphi\,\partial_j G_s\,dS
=
\varphi(\mathbf{r}')\oint n_i\partial_j G_s\,dS
+\mathbf{e}_k\cdot\nabla\varphi(\mathbf{r}')\,\oint n_i R_k\,\partial_j G_s\,dS
+\mathcal{O}(a^{2}),
\]
with $R_k=a\hat{n}_k$.
The leading term uses $dS=a^{2}\,d\Omega$ on $\partial B_a$ and the solid-angle moment
$\int_{4\pi}\hat{n}_i\hat{n}_j\,d\Omega=\frac{4\pi}{3}\delta_{ij}$, so $\oint \hat{n}_i\hat{n}_j\,dS=\frac{4\pi}{3}\delta_{ij}\,a^{2}$, giving
\[
\varphi(\mathbf{r}')\oint n_i\partial_j G_s\,dS
=
-\frac{\varphi(\mathbf{r}')}{4\pi a^2}\oint \hat{n}_i\hat{n}_j\,dS
=
-\frac{\delta_{ij}}{3}\,\varphi(\mathbf{r}').
\]
The linear $\mathbf{R}$ term contracts with an odd integrand $\oint \hat{n}_i\hat{n}_j\hat{n}_k\,dS=0$ by symmetry \cite{jackson1999}.
The $\nabla\varphi$ boundary contribution from the second integration by parts on $V_a$ is
$\mathcal{O}(a)$ and vanishes as $a\to 0$.
Combining volume and contact limits yields Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.grad-grad-PV-pairing}.
The isotropic coefficient $-\delta_{ij}/3$ is geometry-specific: it is the spherical centred source dyad of Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.yaghjian-source-dyad-sphere}, not a universal constant for non-spherical excluded volumes \cite{yaghjian1978,yaghjian1980source}.
Both sides carry dimensions m$^{-3}$, consistent with the scalar delta paired with a second-rank tensor identity.
\end{proof}
For the Helmholtz kernel $G_0=e^{-\jj k_0 R}/(4\pi R)$ under $\eim$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ch2.helmholtz-static-dynamic-split}
G_0(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}')
=
\frac{1}{4\pi R}
+\frac{e^{-\jj k_0 R}-1}{4\pi R},
\end{equation}
where $G_{\mathrm{dyn}}=(e^{-\jj k_0 R}-1)/(4\pi R)$ is $C^{1}$ at $R=0$ (bounded limit $-\jj k_0/(4\pi)$) and $C^{2}$ away from the origin, so $\nabla\nabla G_{\mathrm{dyn}}$ is locally integrable in the punctured sense used below.
Second derivatives of $G_{\mathrm{dyn}}$ therefore contribute at most finitely to interior-sphere fluxes at $\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)$ and never produce a separate $\delta^{(3)}$ tensor under the spherical PV prescription \cite{yaghjian1978,tai1994}.
The contact distribution in Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.grad-grad-1-over-R-distributional} arises from the electrostatic $1/R$ piece alone;
dynamic corrections enter Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.dyadic-inner-sphere-flux-column} through $e^{-\jj k_0\varepsilon}=1-\jj k_0\varepsilon+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{2})$ on $\partial B_\varepsilon$.
The distributional relation Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.grad-grad-1-over-R-distributional} and the geometry-dependent source dyad $\overline{\overline L}$ in the principal-volume field representation are two languages for the same spherical centred contact physics; the macroscopic field is unique when singular convolution and source-dyad terms are counted exactly once \cite{yaghjian1978,yaghjian1980source}.
Physically, excluding a small ball around a discretization cell implements the same regularization a reader expects from reactive near-zone storage: transverse radiation still decouples at large $r$, while longitudinal self-fields remain confined to the source region until $\Pi_{\mathrm{rad}}$ is applied in Section~\ref{sec:ch25}.
\begin{theorem}[Distributional fundamental dyad at coincidence --- vacuum Lorenz construction]
\label{thm:ch2.dyadic-distributional-fundamental}
For the outgoing vacuum kernels $G_0$ and $\overline{\mathbf{G}}_{E,0}$ from
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:ch2.free-space-scalar-green} and~\eqref{eq:ch2.free-space-dyadic-green} under $\eim$,
with $\overline{\mathbf{G}}_{E,0}$ the Lorenz-gauge electric dyad of Proposition~\ref{prop:ch2.dyadic-curl-curl-green},
the scalar identity is Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.scalar-green-distributional} from
Proposition~\ref{prop:ch2.scalar-green-vacuum}, and
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ch2.dyadic-green-distribution}
\mathcal{W}_{E,\omega}\,\overline{\mathbf{G}}_{E,0}(\cdot,\mathbf{r}')
=\overline{\mathbf{I}}\,\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}')
\end{equation}
in $\mathcal{D}'(\mathbb{R}^{3})$, with $\mathcal{W}_{E,\omega}=\nabla\times\nabla\times-k_0^{2}$.
The conclusion holds for test fields $\mathbf{f}\in C_c^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{3}$ and uses the interior-sphere
limit on $V_\varepsilon=B_{R_\varepsilon}\setminus B_\varepsilon(\mathbf{r}')$ with outward normal on
$\partial B_\varepsilon(\mathbf{r}')$ pointing away from $\mathbf{r}'$ \cite{tai1994,stratton1941}.
The interior-sphere limit is the spherical principal-volume prescription of Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.yaghjian-source-dyad-sphere}.
It does not by itself establish coincident evaluation for non-Lorenz dyadic definitions, non-spherical excluded volumes, or layered media.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Homogeneous region.} For $R>0$, Eqs.~\eqref{eq:ch2.scalar-green-distributional} and
Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.dyadic-green-distribution} agree with Proposition~\ref{prop:ch2.scalar-green-vacuum}
and Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.column-curl-curl-check}.
\emph{Distributional action.} Test against $\mathbf{f}\in C_c^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{3}$.
Choose $R_\varepsilon$ so $B_{R_\varepsilon}$ encloses $\operatorname{supp}\mathbf{f}$ and $\mathbf{r}'$.
On $V_\varepsilon=B_{R_\varepsilon}\setminus B_\varepsilon(\mathbf{r}')$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.dyadic-green-ibp} gives
\[
\int_{V_\varepsilon}\mathbf{f}\cdot\mathcal{W}_{E,\omega}\mathbf{G}_{E,0}^{(a)}\,d^{3}\mathbf{r}
=
\int_{V_\varepsilon}\mathbf{G}_{E,0}^{(a)}\cdot\mathcal{W}_{E,\omega}\mathbf{f}\,d^{3}\mathbf{r}
+\oint_{\partial V_\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{\mathcal{S}}[\mathbf{f},\mathbf{G}_{E,0}^{(a)}]\cdot d\mathbf{S},
\]
with $\boldsymbol{\mathcal{S}}$ the vector Green boundary term in Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.dyadic-green-ibp}.
The volume integrand on $V_\varepsilon$ is zero by Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.column-curl-curl-check}.
The exterior sphere $\partial B_{R_\varepsilon}$ contributes $\mathcal{O}(R_\varepsilon^{-2})$ under outgoing
$e^{-\jj k_0 R}/R$ decay and $\mathbf{f}$ fixed on compact support, so its contribution vanishes as $R_\varepsilon\to\infty$.
On the interior sphere $|{\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}'}|=\varepsilon$, expand
$G_0=e^{-\jj k_0\varepsilon}/(4\pi\varepsilon)=1/(4\pi\varepsilon)+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)$.
Green's second identity on $B_\varepsilon$ gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ch2.scalar-interior-sphere-limit}
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}
\oint_{|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}'|=\varepsilon}
\Big(\varphi\,\partial_{n}G_0-G_0\,\partial_{n}\varphi\Big)\,dS
=\varphi(\mathbf{r}'),
\end{equation}
with outward normal $n$ on $\partial B_\varepsilon(\mathbf{r}')$, reproducing the scalar fundamental mass
\cite{jackson1999,stratton1941}.
For the dyadic column $\mathbf{G}_{E,0}^{(a)}$, expand the Lorenz field $\mathbf{E}^{(a)}$ of Step~4 in
Subsection~\ref{sec:ch231} as $\mathbf{G}_{E,0}^{(a)}=\mu_0\!\left[\jj\omega\mathbf{A}^{(a)}+\nabla\phi^{(a)}/(\jj\omega\varepsilon_0)\right]$
with $\mathbf{A}^{(a)}=G_0\mathbf{e}_a$ and $\phi^{(a)}=(\mu_0/\varepsilon_0)\,\partial_a G_0$.
Using $\nabla\times\nabla\times=\nabla\nabla\cdot-\nabla^{2}$ componentwise,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ch2.column-vector-laplacian-split}
\nabla\times\nabla\times\mathbf{G}_{E,0}^{(a)}
=\nabla\big(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{G}_{E,0}^{(a)}\big)-\nabla^{2}\mathbf{G}_{E,0}^{(a)},
\end{equation}
the leading $1/\varepsilon$ flux on $\partial B_\varepsilon(\mathbf{r}')$ comes from the transverse piece
proportional to $G_0\mathbf{e}_a$.
The longitudinal Lorenz piece $\nabla\phi^{(a)}$ expands in $\nabla\nabla G_0$ whose electrostatic part pairs with
Proposition~\ref{prop:ch2.grad-grad-PV-proof}: the isotropic $-\frac{1}{3}\delta_{ij}\delta^{(3)}$ contact is accounted
in the spherical interior-sphere limit together with the transverse flux, not as an extra $\overline{\mathbf{I}}\delta^{(3)}$ pasted onto Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.free-space-dyadic-green}.
Charge continuity $\nabla\cdot\Jimp=-\jj\omega\rho$ pairs the longitudinal term with the scalar identity
Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.scalar-interior-sphere-limit} so that only the vector impulse $\overline{\mathbf{I}}\delta^{(3)}$ remains in
$\mathcal{W}_{E,\omega}\overline{\mathbf{G}}_{E,0}$ \cite{tai1994,stratton1941,yaghjian1978}.
The interior sphere contribution therefore reproduces $f_a(\mathbf{r}')$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.dyadic-green-ibp}, not a separate
longitudinal tensor source.
\emph{Columns $a\in\{x,y,z\}$ on $\partial B_\varepsilon(\mathbf{r}')$.}
On $V_\varepsilon$ the punctured observation point approaches the source only through the excluded sphere
$|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}'|=\varepsilon$.
Take the outward unit normal $\hat{\mathbf{n}}=\widehat{\mathbf{R}}$ on $\partial B_\varepsilon(\mathbf{r}')$ directed from
$\mathbf{r}'$ toward $\mathbf{r}$, so $\mathbf{r}=\mathbf{r}'+\varepsilon\hat{\mathbf{n}}$,
$R=\varepsilon$, $G_0=e^{-\jj k_0\varepsilon}/(4\pi\varepsilon)=1/(4\pi\varepsilon)+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)$, and
$\partial_n G_0=\hat{\mathbf{n}}\cdot\nabla G_0=-1/(4\pi\varepsilon^2)+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)$.
In Lorenz gauge the impressed columns satisfy $\mathbf{A}^{(a)}=G_0\mathbf{e}_a$ and
$\mathbf{G}_{E,0}^{(a)}=\mu_0\jj\omega\,G_0\mathbf{e}_a+\nabla\phi^{(a)}/(\jj\omega\varepsilon_0)$ with
$\phi^{(a)}=(\mu_0/\varepsilon_0)\partial_a G_0$ \cite{stratton1941,tai1994}.
The reactive gradients of $\phi^{(a)}$ contribute only to $\nabla(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{G}_{E,0}^{(a)})$ in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.column-vector-laplacian-split}; their interior-sphere flux is $\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)$ when paired with smooth
$\mathbf{f}$ and the electrostatic contact tensor is accounted through Proposition~\ref{prop:ch2.grad-grad-PV-proof}, not as an
extra $\overline{\mathbf{I}}\delta^{(3)}$ pasted onto Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.free-space-dyadic-green}.
At leading order $\mathbf{G}_{E,0}^{(a)}\sim\mu_0\jj\omega\,G_0\mathbf{e}_a$ and the $R^{-2},R^{-3}$ pieces in
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:ch2.dyadic-column-x-explicit} and the cyclic $y$-, $z$-columns are subleading on $\partial B_\varepsilon$.

\emph{Column $a=x$.}
Equation~\eqref{eq:ch2.dyadic-column-x-explicit} gives, at leading order on $\partial B_\varepsilon$,
$\mathbf{G}_{E,0}^{(x)}\sim\mu_0\jj\omega\,G_0\mathbf{e}_x$ with $G_0\sim(4\pi\varepsilon)^{-1}$.
For $\mathbf{f}=\mathbf{e}_x f$ with $f$ smooth near $\mathbf{r}'$,
\[
\mathbf{f}\cdot\partial_n\mathbf{G}_{E,0}^{(x)}
-\partial_n\mathbf{f}\cdot\mathbf{G}_{E,0}^{(x)}
\sim
\frac{f(\mathbf{r}')}{4\pi\varepsilon^{2}},
\]
because $\mathbf{e}_x\cdot\mathbf{e}_x=1$ and $\partial_n\mathbf{f}$ is bounded on the shrinking sphere.
Substituting $\mathcal{W}_{E,\omega}\mathbf{f}=\mathbf{e}_x$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.dyadic-green-ibp} gives flux
$f_x(\mathbf{r}')$ after $\oint dS=4\pi\varepsilon^2$, with the $\mu_0\jj\omega$ scale carried consistently through
$\boldsymbol{\mathcal{S}}$ as in the scalar limit Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.scalar-interior-sphere-limit}.

\emph{Column $a=y$.}
By the same steps with $\mathbf{e}_y$ in place of $\mathbf{e}_x$,
\[
\mathbf{f}\cdot\partial_n\mathbf{G}_{E,0}^{(y)}
-\partial_n\mathbf{f}\cdot\mathbf{G}_{E,0}^{(y)}
\sim
\frac{f(\mathbf{r}')}{4\pi\varepsilon^{2}},
\qquad \mathbf{f}=\mathbf{e}_y f,
\]
yielding $f_y(\mathbf{r}')$ in the inner-sphere pairing.

\emph{Column $a=z$.}
With $\mathbf{f}=\mathbf{e}_z f$,
\[
\mathbf{f}\cdot\partial_n\mathbf{G}_{E,0}^{(z)}
-\partial_n\mathbf{f}\cdot\mathbf{G}_{E,0}^{(z)}
\sim
\frac{f(\mathbf{r}')}{4\pi\varepsilon^{2}}
\]
at leading order.
Therefore
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ch2.dyadic-inner-sphere-flux-column}
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}
\oint_{|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}'|=\varepsilon}
\Big(\mathbf{f}\cdot\partial_{n}\mathbf{G}_{E,0}^{(a)}
-\partial_{n}\mathbf{f}\cdot\mathbf{G}_{E,0}^{(a)}\Big)\,dS
=f_a(\mathbf{r}'),
\qquad a\in\{x,y,z\},
\end{equation}
with $\partial_{n}=\hat{\mathbf{n}}\cdot\nabla$ and the integrand the same $\boldsymbol{\mathcal{S}}$ pairing as in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.dyadic-green-ibp} \cite{jackson1999,stratton1941}.
Equation~\eqref{eq:ch2.dyadic-inner-sphere-flux-column} is the vector analogue of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.scalar-interior-sphere-limit}: the $1/\varepsilon$ part of $G_0$ forces the $1/\varepsilon^2$
normal flux of the impressed column, while $\partial_{n}\mathbf{f}$ stays bounded on the fixed test function.
Summing the three Cartesian column pairings yields
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ch2.dyadic-test-pairing-columns}
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{r})\cdot\mathcal{W}_{E,\omega}\mathbf{G}_{E,0}^{(a)}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}')\,d^{3}\mathbf{r}
=f_a(\mathbf{r}'),
\qquad a\in\{x,y,z\},
\end{equation}
so $\mathcal{W}_{E,\omega}\overline{\mathbf{G}}_{E,0}=\overline{\mathbf{I}}\delta^{(3)}$ in $\mathcal{D}'$ \cite{stratton1941,jackson1999}.
\emph{Componentwise column $a=x$.} With $\mathbf{R}=\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}'$, $\widehat{\mathbf{R}}=\mathbf{R}/R$, and
$f(\mathbf{r})\in C_c^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.free-space-dyadic-green} defines the $x$-directed
impressed column by left action on $\mathbf{e}_x$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ch2.dyadic-column-x-explicit}
\mathbf{G}_{E,0}^{(x)}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}')
=
\overline{\mathbf{G}}_{E,0}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}')\cdot\mathbf{e}_x
=
\frac{e^{-\jj k_0 R}}{4\pi R}
\left[
\mathbf{e}_x-\widehat{\mathbf{R}}\big(\widehat{\mathbf{R}}\cdot\mathbf{e}_x\big)
+\left(\frac{1}{\jj k_0 R}-\frac{1}{k_0^{2}R^{2}}\right)
\Big(\mathbf{e}_x-3\,\widehat{\mathbf{R}}\big(\widehat{\mathbf{R}}\cdot\mathbf{e}_x\big)\Big)
\right],
\qquad R>0,
\end{equation}
with analogous columns $\mathbf{G}_{E,0}^{(y)}=\overline{\mathbf{G}}_{E,0}\cdot\mathbf{e}_y$ and
$\mathbf{G}_{E,0}^{(z)}=\overline{\mathbf{G}}_{E,0}\cdot\mathbf{e}_z$ \cite{stratton1941,tai1994}.
For $R>0$, apply $\mathcal{W}_{E,\omega}=\nabla\times\nabla\times-k_0^{2}$ at the observation point:
$\nabla\times\nabla\times\mathbf{G}_{E,0}^{(x)}=\nabla(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{G}_{E,0}^{(x)})-\nabla^{2}\mathbf{G}_{E,0}^{(x)}$.
Each Cartesian piece is a linear combination of $G_0$ and its gradients; away from the singularity
$(\nabla^{2}+k_0^{2})G_0=0$, and the longitudinal $\nabla(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{G}_{E,0}^{(x)})$ cancels the
remaining $k_0^{2}$ term in Lorenz form \cite{tai1994,stratton1941}, so
$\mathcal{W}_{E,\omega}\mathbf{G}_{E,0}^{(x)}=\mathbf{0}$ as in Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.column-curl-curl-check}.
At $R\to 0^{+}$, only the transverse $e^{-\jj k_0 R}/(4\pi R)$ part of $\mathbf{G}_{E,0}^{(x)}$ contributes the
$1/\varepsilon$ flux in Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.dyadic-inner-sphere-flux-column}; the $R^{-2}$ and $R^{-3}$ reactive terms
are subleading on $\partial B_\varepsilon(\mathbf{r}')$ and do not add a second distributional tensor source
\cite{tai1994,jackson1999}. For $a=y,z$ the same interior-sphere flux reproduces $f_y,f_z$, completing the three-column
pairing \cite{tai1994}. Layered media or non-Lorenz gauge dyads remain outside the present theorem.
Columns $a=y,z$ follow by cyclic permutation of Cartesian labels.
\end{proof}
Physically, the $\delta^{(3)}$ term is the idealized impressed dipole source; finite patches regularize the same mass over
$\operatorname{supp}\Jimp$ rather than a mathematical point \cite{stratton1941}.
Physically, the distinction among (i)--(iii) is sharp: the tabulated dyad gives the field at
observation points separated from the source; the distributional identity specifies how the kernel
must be tested when $\mathbf{r}\to\mathbf{r}'$; the principal-volume convolution is the
finite-ball exclusion prescription required before Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.electric-dyadic-convolution}

The longitudinal contact in Proposition~\ref{prop:ch2.grad-grad-PV-proof} is the price of differentiating the $1/R$ Coulomb potential
inside the source region: it is reactive, not radiative, and cancels in far-zone flux meters that select outgoing transverse content
through $\Pi_{\mathrm{rad}}$ in Chapter~\ref{ch:02} and Section~\ref{sec:ch24}.
For \(\mathbf{r}\notin\operatorname{supp}\Jimp\), the convolution
Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.electric-dyadic-convolution} is a classical integral. What happens when
observation and source points become arbitrarily close? The free-space dyad develops a
non-integrable leading singularity: field amplitudes grow as inverse powers of separation
$R$, reflecting reactive storage and self-interaction between neighboring source elements
\cite{stratton1941}. These singularities do not imply infinite radiated power---they signal
that pointwise source models and naive quadrature break down, while extended patches with
finite extent carry finite reactive energy \cite{jackson1999}. As
\(\mathbf{r}\to\mathbf{r}'\in\operatorname{supp}\Jimp\), the free-space dyad
Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.free-space-dyadic-green} develops a non-integrable leading singularity;
the local expansion in powers of \(k_0 R\) separates static storage from dynamic radiation
and is the analytic starting point for every regularization scheme in the display below.
With separation vector \(\mathbf{R}=\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}'\), distance \(R=|\mathbf{R}|\) in
metres, observation point $\mathbf{r}$ and source point $\mathbf{r}'$, and unit direction
\(\widehat{\mathbf{R}}=\mathbf{R}/R\),

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ch2.dyadic-singular-expansion}
\overline{\mathbf{G}}_{E,0}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}')
=
\frac{1}{4\pi R^{3}}\,\overline{\mathbf{P}}_{t}(\widehat{\mathbf{R}})
\;+\;
\frac{\jj k_0}{4\pi R^{2}}\,\overline{\mathbf{Q}}(\widehat{\mathbf{R}})
\;+\;
\frac{k_0^{2}}{4\pi R}\,\overline{\mathbf{S}}(\widehat{\mathbf{R}})
\;+\;\mathcal{O}(k_0^{3}),
\end{equation}
where \(\overline{\mathbf{P}}_{t}(\widehat{\mathbf{R}})=\overline{\mathbf{I}}-3\widehat{\mathbf{R}}\widehat{\mathbf{R}}\)
is transverse to \(\mathbf{R}\) and \(\overline{\mathbf{Q}}\), \(\overline{\mathbf{S}}\)
collect the next two anisotropic terms in the locked phasor convention
\cite{harrington2001}. Equation~\eqref{eq:ch2.dyadic-singular-expansion} matches the
\(R^{-3}\), \(R^{-2}\), and \(R^{-1}\) pieces of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.free-space-dyadic-green} and separates reactive near storage from
radiative \(R^{-1}\) decay. The \(R^{-3}\) term is non-integrable in a neighborhood
of coincidence; convolution with \(\Jimp\) therefore requires a distributional or
regularized definition, not pointwise Riemann integration
\cite{stratton1941}. The scalar kernel Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.free-space-scalar-green}
carries the parallel ordering \(R^{-1}\), \(R^{0}\), \ldots\ with
\(R^{-1}\) non-integrable on neighborhoods of the source.
In the electrostatic limit \(k_0\to 0\) at fixed separation \(R>0\) (metres), the dynamic
terms in Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.dyadic-singular-expansion} vanish and only the \(R^{-3}\)
dipolar projector survives; that static piece is extracted analytically in
integral-equation codes before the smooth remainder is integrated numerically.
Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.dyadic-singular-expansion} reduces to
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ch2.static-dyadic-limit}
\overline{\mathbf{G}}_{E,0}^{\mathrm{(st)}}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}')
=
\frac{1}{4\pi R^{3}}\Big(\overline{\mathbf{I}}-3\,\widehat{\mathbf{R}}\widehat{\mathbf{R}}\Big),
\end{equation}
the field of a unit dipole aligned with each Cartesian source direction
\cite{jackson1999}. Physically, Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.static-dyadic-limit} captures static-field
dominance at small $k_0 R$: reactive storage and self-interaction between neighboring
current elements overwhelm the radiative $R^{-1}$ term until separation or frequency
increases \cite{stratton1941}. Equation~\eqref{eq:ch2.static-dyadic-limit} is the
leading singular part extracted in integral-equation static--dynamic splits: dynamic
corrections are \(\mathcal{O}(k_0 R)\) relative to the static term when
\(k_0 R\ll 1\) on source patches \cite{harrington2001}. For divergence-free
\(\Jimp\), the \(\grad\,\divg\) structure of Lorenz reduction
Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.lorenz-field-from-potential} removes monopole artifacts; the
dominant self-coupling on compact patches is dipolar and transverse to the separation
vector between observation and source centroids \cite{stratton1941}.

Write

\mathcal{S}_\omega

compact remainder suitable for stable numerical quadrature, and

When \(\Jimp\) is a bounded function, Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.electric-dyadic-convolution}
defines \(\E\in L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)^{3}\) away from the source. For sheet
currents Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.distributional-source-extension}, the convolution is defined
by duality: for all smooth compactly supported test fields \(\boldsymbol{\phi}\) (dimensionless
weighting fields that probe how the electric field pairs against admissible test states),
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ch2.distributional-dyadic-pairing}
\langle\E,\boldsymbol{\phi}\rangle_{\Omega}
=
-\jj\omega\mu_0
\sum_{ab}\int_{\Sigma_{ab}}\int_{\Omega}
\mathbf{J}_{s,ab}(\mathbf{r}')
\cdot\overline{\mathbf{G}}_{E}^{\mathsf{T}}(\mathbf{r}',\mathbf{r})\cdot\boldsymbol{\phi}(\mathbf{r})\,d^{3}r\,dS',
\end{equation}
Here $\boldsymbol{\phi}$ is a smooth test field used to probe the electric field when
pointwise evaluation fails; $\Sigma_{ab}$ is a surface patch carrying sheet current
$\mathbf{J}_{s,ab}$ (A\,m\(^{-1}\)), the surface density of impressed current on patch
$(a,b)$; and $\mathbf{J}_{s,ab}(\mathbf{r}')$ is the physical current flowing on that
patch at source point $\mathbf{r}'$ \cite{stratton1941}, with appropriate Fubini interpretation and outgoing radiation condition on exterior problems
\cite{stratton1941}. Equation~\eqref{eq:ch2.distributional-dyadic-pairing} is the
weak form of Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.electric-dyadic-convolution} when pointwise evaluation
fails; it pairs with Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.maxwell-weak-form} once \(\boldsymbol{\phi}\)
is replaced by admissible test states in \(\mathcal{F}_\Omega^{0}\)
\cite{harrington2001}. Physically, sheet sources produce jump conditions on
\(\hat{\mathbf{n}}\times\E\) and \(\hat{\mathbf{n}}\times\H\); the distributional
definition ensures those jumps arise from the same kernel as volume currents, not from
ad hoc post-factors \cite{stratton1941}.
For volume \(\Jimp\) and observation points \(\mathbf{r}\notin\operatorname{supp}\Jimp\)
approaching the support, one may attempt to define the field as the symmetric limit of
integrals that exclude a small ball about the observation point. The principal value is that
symmetric exclusion limit: ordinary integration fails because the $R^{-3}$ pole is not
integrable over a neighborhood of coincidence, but symmetric cancellation about the
observation point can still yield a finite field when the singularity is weak enough
\cite{jackson1999}. Define the principal-value convolution
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ch2.principal-value-convolution}
\E(\mathbf{r})
=
-\jj\omega\mu_0\,\mathrm{p.v.}\int_{\Omega}
\overline{\mathbf{G}}_{E}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}')\cdot\Jimp(\mathbf{r}')\,d^{3}r',
\end{equation}
as the limit of integrals over \(\Omega\setminus B_{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{r})\) as
\(\varepsilon\downarrow 0\) when that limit exists in \(\HCurl(\Omega)\)
\cite{jackson1999}. For observation on the source support---for example
\(\mathbf{r}\in\Sigma_{ab}\) for a surface patch---the principal value generally
diverges because of the \(R^{-3}\) term; the \emph{Hadamard finite part} subtracts
the singular expansion explicitly and retains the finite reactive coupling between
extended patches. Finite-part subtraction works because the non-integrable piece is known
analytically from Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.dyadic-singular-expansion} and can be removed before
the remainder is integrated numerically---the engineering prescription integral-equation codes implement
as static--dynamic splitting \cite{harrington2001}:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ch2.finite-part-convolution}
\E(\mathbf{r})
=
-\jj\omega\mu_0\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}
\left[
\int_{\Omega\setminus B_{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{r})}
\overline{\mathbf{G}}_{E}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}')\cdot\Jimp(\mathbf{r}')\,d^{3}r'
\;+\;
\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{r})\cap\Omega}
\overline{\mathbf{G}}_{E}^{\mathrm{(sing)}}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}')\cdot\Jimp(\mathbf{r}')\,d^{3}r'
\right],
\end{equation}
provided \(\Jimp\) is smooth enough on the patch that the limit exists
\cite{harrington2001}. Equation~\eqref{eq:ch2.finite-part-convolution} is the
prescription for on-surface field evaluation; it differs
from Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.principal-value-convolution} by the explicit analytic
subtraction of \(\overline{\mathbf{G}}_{E}^{\mathrm{(sing)}}\), not by numerical
finer spatial resolution alone \cite{stratton1941}. Mathematically, the finite part is the
constant term in the Laurent expansion of the integral as \(\varepsilon\to 0\);
physically, it removes infinite self-energy of pointwise source models while retaining
finite reactive coupling between extended patches \cite{jackson1999}.
Integral-equation and boundary-element codes replace the continuum convolution
Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.electric-dyadic-convolution} by discrete impedance entries obtained
by testing the dyadic kernel against pairs of basis functions on source patches. In plain
language: $\mathbf{f}_m$ and $\mathbf{f}_n$ are the $m$th and $n$th current basis functions
on patches $\Omega_m$ and $\Omega_n$; $\Omega_m$ and $\Omega_n$ are the surface or volume
patches where those basis functions live; and $Z_{mn}$ is the mutual impedance---the
complex voltage response at patch $m$ per unit current on patch $n$---that a numerical code
assembles from the double integral below \cite{harrington2001}. What a integral-equation code actually
computes is this bilinear pairing of basis functions through the dyadic propagator, not a
pointwise field evaluation at patch centers unless a separate approximation is introduced.
The Galerkin entry for patches \(\Omega_m\) and \(\Omega_n\) reads
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ch2.mom-dyadic-entry}
Z_{mn}
=
-\jj\omega\mu_0\int_{\Omega_m}\int_{\Omega_n}
\mathbf{f}_m(\mathbf{r})\cdot
\overline{\mathbf{G}}_{E}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}')\cdot
\mathbf{f}_n(\mathbf{r}')\,d^{3}r\,d^{3}r',
\end{equation}
for basis functions \(\mathbf{f}_m,\mathbf{f}_n\) on patches \(\Omega_m,\Omega_n\)
\cite{harrington2001}. When \(m=n\), the integrand is non-integrable at
\(\mathbf{r}=\mathbf{r}'\); the Chapter~\ref{ch:02} regularized self-term is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ch2.mom-self-term-regularized}
Z_{mm}^{\mathrm{(reg)}}
=
-\jj\omega\mu_0\int_{\Omega_m}\int_{\Omega_m}
\mathbf{f}_m(\mathbf{r})\cdot
\overline{\mathbf{G}}_{E}^{\mathrm{(reg)}}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}')\cdot
\mathbf{f}_m(\mathbf{r}')\,d^{3}r\,d^{3}r'
\;+\;
Z_{mm}^{\mathrm{(sing)}},
\end{equation}
where $Z_{mm}^{\mathrm{(reg)}}$ is the numerically integrated smooth remainder,
$Z_{mm}^{\mathrm{(sing)}}$ is the analytic integral of
\(\overline{\mathbf{G}}_{E}^{\mathrm{(sing)}}\) over the coincident patch---often
reduced to closed forms for flat triangles and rectangles---and the sum is the quantity a
code must assemble so that neighboring discretization elements do not produce near-singular divergences
when observation and source points approach one another \cite{stratton1941}. Point
matching---evaluating Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.electric-dyadic-convolution} at patch centers
with \(\overline{\mathbf{G}}_{E}\) replaced by
\(\overline{\mathbf{G}}_{E}^{\mathrm{(reg)}}\) only---is \emph{not} equivalent to
Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.mom-self-term-regularized} unless basis overlap and singular
subtraction match the same finite-part convention \cite{harrington2001}. The
distinction is one of source realizability: Galerkin testing defines a bounded bilinear form on
\(\mathcal{J}_\Omega\times\mathcal{J}_\Omega\); raw point evaluation does not
\cite{stratton1941}.
When \(\mathbf{r}\) and \(\mathbf{r}'\) lie on distinct patches separated by small
distance \(d\ll \lambda_0=2\pi/k_0\), direct quadrature of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.free-space-dyadic-green} is ill-conditioned. Physically, neighboring mesh
elements interact through nearly coincident kernel samples: the separation is small enough
that reactive $R^{-3}$ and $R^{-2}$ terms vary rapidly across the integration domain, and
standard quadrature underestimates or overestimates the coupling unless the singular part is
subtracted and integrated analytically \cite{harrington2001}. The standard
subtraction template is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ch2.near-singular-subtraction}
\int_{\Omega_n}
\overline{\mathbf{G}}_{E}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}')\cdot\Jimp(\mathbf{r}')\,d^{3}r'
=
\int_{\Omega_n}
\Big[\overline{\mathbf{G}}_{E}-\overline{\mathbf{G}}_{E}^{\mathrm{(near)}}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}')\Big]
\cdot\Jimp(\mathbf{r}')\,d^{3}r'
\;+\;
\int_{\Omega_n}
\overline{\mathbf{G}}_{E}^{\mathrm{(near)}}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}')\cdot\Jimp(\mathbf{r}')\,d^{3}r',
\end{equation}
where \(\overline{\mathbf{G}}_{E}^{\mathrm{(near)}}\) matches the first few terms of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.dyadic-singular-expansion} about \(\mathbf{r}\) and the integral of
\(\overline{\mathbf{G}}_{E}^{\mathrm{(near)}}\) is evaluated analytically
\cite{harrington2001}. Equation~\eqref{eq:ch2.near-singular-subtraction}
does not alter the continuum operator; it stabilizes numerical approximation of
\(\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{cpt}}\) in Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.solution-singular-compact-split}.
Scalar singularities are weaker than dyadic singularities: the monopole kernel diverges only
as $R^{-1}$, which is barely non-integrable in three dimensions, whereas the dyadic kernel
carries an $R^{-3}$ pole that breaks naive integration more severely \cite{jackson1999}. This
distinction matters computationally because scalar potential formulations can sometimes use
simpler self-term corrections, while vector integral-equation codes must implement full static--dynamic
dyadic subtraction \cite{harrington2001}. The scalar Green function satisfies, in distributional sense,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ch2.scalar-coincidence-limit}
\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{r})}
G_0(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}')\,d^{3}r'
=
\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
\;+\;\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{3}),
\end{equation}
for small balls \(B_{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{r})\) in \(\mathbb{R}^{3}\), reflecting the
\(R^{-1}\) integrability borderline in three dimensions \cite{jackson1999}. Equation
\eqref{eq:ch2.scalar-coincidence-limit} explains why monopole self-terms in scalar
potential formulations require either finite source extent or explicit regularization;
vector dyads inherit stronger \(R^{-3}\) divergence captured by
Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.static-dyadic-limit} \cite{stratton1941}.
Equations~\eqref{eq:ch2.finite-part-convolution}--\eqref{eq:ch2.mom-self-term-regularized}
require patchwise smooth \(\Jimp\), Lipschitz \(\Omega\), and fixed \(\omega\) in the
passive linear band. They fail for point dipoles modeled as \(\delta\)-supported
\(\Jimp\) without accompanying finite-part renormalization, and for non-Lipschitz
edges where \(\HCurl\) membership of \(\E\) requires additional edge singularities not captured by Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch2.dyadic-singular-expansion}
\cite{stratton1941}. Lossy media with complex \(k_0\) shift singular
expansion coefficients but not the order of the \(R^{-3}\) pole; Subsection~\ref{sec:ch232}
does not re-tabulate complex-\(k\) subtractors \cite{stratton1941}. Regularized convolutions
now make $\mathcal{S}_\omega[\Jimp]$ well-defined when observation $\mathbf{r}$ approaches source
support; retarded time-domain support fixes \emph{when} those spatial kernels may act, completing
the causal picture begun in the harmonic dyads with $\mathbf{r}$ as observation and $\mathbf{r}'$
as source \cite{stratton1941}.
