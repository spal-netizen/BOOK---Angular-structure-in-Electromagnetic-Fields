\subsection{Admissibility and the export chain}
\label{sec:ch203}

A radiation problem is specified by impressed sources, constitutive laws on subdomains, boundary traces on $\Gamma=\partial\Omega$, and---when the exterior is unbounded---an outgoing selection at infinity compatible with Chapter~\ref{ch:01} \cite{stratton1941,maxwell1873}. The harmonic solution map $\mathcal{S}_\omega$ assigns to each admissible impressed current $\Jimp\in\mathcal{J}_\Omega$ the unique Maxwell field pair $(\E,\H)\in\mathcal{F}_\Omega$ that satisfies Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch1.maxwell-local} together with that data tuple. Admissibility is not a symbolic predicate: it is the simultaneous satisfaction of square-integrable source support (when that class is declared), charge continuity
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ch2.charge-continuity-admissibility}
\divg\Jimp+\jj\omega\rho=0
\end{equation}
in the sense of distributions on $\Omega$, compatible boundary conditions on $\Gamma$, and the exterior radiation condition developed in Section~\ref{sec:ch24} \cite{stratton1941}.

The composition $\mathcal{R}_\omega=\Pi_{\mathrm{rad}}\circ\mathcal{S}_\omega$ is defined only after $\mathcal{S}_\omega$ is established as a solution map and $\Pi_{\mathrm{rad}}$ is defined as the map that associates to a completed pair its Sommerfeld-outgoing far-zone coefficients (Section~\ref{sec:ch201}; refinement in Section~\ref{sec:ch242}). Far-zone power, pattern, and reciprocity integrals therefore attach to $\mathcal{R}_\omega[\Jimp]$ or to equivalent asymptotic data, not to arbitrary components of $\mathcal{S}_\omega[\Jimp]$ that decay faster than $1/r$ or carry reactive phase on a finite shell \cite{poynting1884,harrington2001}. Chapter~\ref{ch:01} already separated radiative and reactive flux on enclosing spheres through the complex Poynting vector; the present chapter implements that separation on the operator side by restricting observables to $\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{rad}}$.

Finite instruments realize linear functionals on fields or on asymptotic amplitudes. If $\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{far}}$ extracts a declared far-zone quantity from $\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{rad}}$ and $\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{meas}}$ models a probe bank of rank at most $N$, the laboratory readout is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ch2.operational-measurement-chain}
\Lambda_{\mathrm{lab}}
=
\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{meas}}\circ\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{far}}\circ\mathcal{R}_\omega.
\end{equation}
Linearity of $\mathcal{S}_\omega$, $\mathcal{R}_\omega$, and the observation functionals makes $\Lambda_{\mathrm{lab}}$ a linear map on $\mathcal{J}_\Omega$ whose range has dimension at most $N$ when $\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{meas}}$ has finite rank. That rank limitation is independent of Maxwell's laws: it records how many independent readings a given instrument geometry supplies \cite{hansen1988,stratton1941}.

Rotational covariance of radiation patterns, when the structure and boundary class are invariant under $\mathrm{SO}(3)$, is tested on $\mathcal{R}_\omega[\Jimp]$ rather than on unreconstructed near fields. Passive rotation of coordinates acts on vector components and on the spatial argument as in Chapter~\ref{ch:01}; the radiated power through a large sphere invariant under such rotations when sources and bodies rotate together \cite{stratton1941}. Section~\ref{sec:ch21} supplies the function spaces, traces, and adjoints in which $\mathcal{S}_\omega$ and $\mathcal{R}_\omega$ are studied quantitatively; Section~\ref{sec:ch23} derives the Green kernels that realize $\mathcal{S}_\omega$ in free-space exteriors.
